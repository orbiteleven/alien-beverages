# Slow Rotation

## Description
The Brinehouse's after-hours pour: old cask kelpwine warmed by hand around the glass, deepened with vent resin and burnt sugar until it drinks like something twice its strength. Rich, smoky, bittersweet, and unhurried — the name is a Europan joke about their tidally locked view and also an instruction. This is the drink the bartender makes when the crowd thins and someone's earned a story. It does not travel and it does not rush.

## Method
1. Shave a sliver of smoked vent resin into a mixing glass with a bar spoon of burnt ballast sugar.
2. Add 10ml warm glacier melt and muddle until the sugar slicks.
3. Add 80ml cask-aged kelpwine and 2 dashes Tannhäuser bitters — no ice.
4. Stir five slow minutes at room temperature; the resin gives up its smoke grudgingly.
5. Strain into a ballast tumbler and cup the bowl in your palm a moment before serving. Garnish with a resin shard.

## Bartender's notes
Yes, five minutes, and yes, no ice — this is a warm drink pretending to be a cocktail. The stir is where the resin dissolves; cut it short and you serve sweet wine with a rock in it. Use your oldest kelpwine; the young stuff has nothing to say for five minutes.

Sweeter: a second spoon of sugar, muddled properly at the start, never stirred in late. Drier: skip the sugar and double the bitters, which turns it stern and funereal — some nights that's the order.
