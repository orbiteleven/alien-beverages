# The Long Haul

## Description
Smoke first, salt second, and a whisper of flowers on the way down — the Long Haul tastes like the last hour of a double shift. It's the standing order of freighter crews signing off a six-month run, drunk slow while the paperwork clears. Nobody orders a second in a hurry; the drink punishes rushing. It pairs best with silence and a docked ship.

## Method
1. Combine 50ml Ganymede rye analogue, 10ml Callisto brine, and 15ml thresher kelp syrup in a mixing tin over cracked ice.
2. Add 5ml cold-pressed void bloom.
3. Stir 30 slow rotations — the syrup wants time to marry the brine.
4. Strain into a chilled ballast tumbler over one large cube.
5. Lay the charred kelp ribbon across the rim.

## Bartender's notes
If you're out of cold-pressed void bloom, a short pour of hullmoss distillate gets you most of the way — greener, less perfume, but the smoke still carries it. Don't substitute the brine. Cheap vat salt water reads flat and dead, and the whole drink is built on that mineral edge.

For a drier pour, cut the kelp syrup to 10ml and add two dashes of Tannhäuser bitters. Sweeter palate, go 20ml syrup, but stop there — past that it drinks like engine coolant with ambitions.
