# Mudfoot

## Description
"Mudfoot" is what Europa crews call anyone who's never left their home rock, and the drink is just as unhurried: cask-aged kelpwine cut with cold glacier melt, sweetened with a little kelp syrup, left alone to be itself. Earthy, low-proof, mellow to a fault. It's the session drink of the Brinehouse — you can hold three of these across an evening of cards and still count your chips. The frozen brine pearl melts slowly and salts the last third.

## Method
1. Fill a dock jar two-thirds with ice.
2. Pour 100ml cask-aged kelpwine over.
3. Add 10ml kelp syrup and 30ml pressed glacier melt.
4. Stir three turns, drop in one frozen brine pearl.

## Bartender's notes
The pearl is the trick — don't skip it. It reads as a garnish but it's really a slow-release seasoning, and the drink is designed around that late salt. If you're out of pearls, 5ml of any clean brine stirred in works, but the drink stops evolving in the glass and gets a little boring.

Young kelpwine (under a year in cask) is too sharp for this — save it for a Rust Bucket. Sweeter palate: 15ml syrup. Drier: skip the syrup entirely and let the cask do the talking; that version has its partisans and they tip well.
