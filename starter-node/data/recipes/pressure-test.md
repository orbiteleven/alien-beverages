# Pressure Test

## Description
Built under pressure and served the same way — the flute is charged until the cork gasket sighs, and the first sip snaps like an airlock check. Clean vat spirit, a saline thread, and carbonation wound up tight. Highgate's riggers order it after inspection days, when everything held. Crisp, cold, and a little theatrical, it's the closest thing the Elevator has to a celebration in a glass.

## Method
1. Chill 45ml triple-scrubbed vat ethanol and 12ml rimefrost cordial in a sealed shaker, no ice.
2. Add 1 dash Ionian saltgrass tincture.
3. Charge with 90ml carbonated glacier melt and seal the pressure flute immediately.
4. Rest 60 seconds under pressure, then crack the seal at the table.
5. Drop in a frozen brine pearl.

## Bartender's notes
The tableside crack is the entire show — build it at the bar and you've served an expensive soda. Gaskets wear out; a flute that won't hold pressure makes a flat, sad drink, so test your glassware like the riggers test their rigs.

Drier: skip the cordial and lean on the pearl for interest. Sweeter: 18ml cordial, no more, or the snap goes mushy. If the carbonated melt has been open more than a shift, it's dead — use it for the plants.
