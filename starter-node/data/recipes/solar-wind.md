# Solar Wind

## Description
Served on the Highgate observation deck where the glass walls face sunward, the Solar Wind is all lift: white synthol carrying a few drops of voidbloom extract, charged hard with carbonated glacier melt. Dry, floral, and so effervescent it practically evacuates the flute on its own. The static sugar rim crackles faintly against your lip — the kitchen swears that's physics, not theater. It is absolutely theater.

## Method
1. Rim a pressure flute with static sugar.
2. In a chilled tin, combine 40ml white synthol and 3 drops voidbloom extract — no ice.
3. Add 90ml carbonated glacier melt down the side of the tin.
4. One gentle lift with the spoon, then pour slowly into the flute.

## Bartender's notes
Everything cold, nothing shaken. Carbonation is a budget you spend once — every rough movement bleeds it, and a flat Solar Wind is just floral water with a crunchy rim. Build it faster than the bubbles can escape and serve it walking.

The extract is potent; three drops perfumes 130ml of drink. If a guest wants it "more floral," resist the urge to add drops — swap the sugar rim for a mist of cold-pressed void bloom on top instead. Drier isn't really possible; it's already a desert with bubbles.
