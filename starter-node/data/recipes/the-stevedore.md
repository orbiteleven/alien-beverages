# The Stevedore

## Description
Dry as manifest paper and about as sentimental. Dockgrain spirit stirred cold with a bare minimum of static sugar, a hard bitter line from the Tannhäuser, and a saline finish that shows up late and stays. It's what the cargo chiefs at Highgate drink at end of shift — one, seated, in silence, like a ritual. The candied hullmoss is the only soft thing about it.

## Method
1. Add 60ml Ceres dockgrain spirit to a mixing glass with a bar spoon of static sugar.
2. Stir dry until the sugar dissolves, about 15 seconds.
3. Add ice, 5ml Callisto-grade brine, and 3 dashes Tannhäuser bitters.
4. Stir another 30 seconds — you want real dilution; this drink is raw without it.
5. Strain into a chilled ballast tumbler, no ice. Garnish with the candied hullmoss sprig.

## Bartender's notes
Dissolve the sugar before the ice goes in or it never dissolves at all — static sugar seizes in the cold and you'll serve grit. The brine is a seasoning here, not a flavor; if a customer can name it, you poured double.

There's no sweet version of a Stevedore worth defending, but for a gentler entry use 50ml spirit and stir longer. Substituting Vesta pale grain for the dockgrain works in a pinch — cleaner, less character, the chiefs will notice and say nothing, which is worse.
